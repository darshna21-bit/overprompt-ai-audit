# USER_INTERVIEWS.md

Three conversations conducted during the build week (May 9–12, 2026) with potential users. Each interview lasted approximately 10–15 minutes and was conducted over WhatsApp voice call or in person. Names anonymized on request. Participants were sourced through academic, professional, and personal networks.

---

## Interview 1 — Startup Founder, May 9

**Background:** Founder of a small SaaS startup with a team of 7 people. The company uses ChatGPT Team, Cursor, Claude, GitHub Copilot, and Notion AI across development, customer support, and marketing workflows. Responsible for approving and managing software/tooling expenses.

**Quotes:**

> "AI subscriptions started small, but now we're paying for so many overlapping tools that I honestly don't know which ones are worth keeping."

> "Different team members prefer different AI tools, so we end up subscribing to multiple products that solve almost the same problem."

> "If a product could quickly show where we're overspending without needing complicated setup, I'd absolutely use it."

**Most surprising thing:** The founder estimated that several AI subscriptions remained active for months simply because nobody reviewed usage regularly. Tool spending had become difficult to track as the team scaled.

**What it changed about my design:** Reinforced the importance of showing clear cost breakdowns and actionable recommendations immediately after the audit. It also motivated the addition of total monthly savings visibility and “best alternative plan” suggestions for small startup teams.

## Interview 2 — Software Engineer at Google, May 10

**Background:** Works as a software engineer and regularly uses AI coding tools like Cursor, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and GitHub Copilot for development workflows and productivity tasks.

**Quotes:**

> "Most people just subscribe to multiple AI tools without checking if they're actually using all of them enough to justify the cost."

> "I know engineers paying for Cursor, ChatGPT Plus, Claude, and GitHub Copilot together. Half the time they overlap."

> "If a tool instantly showed me where money is being wasted without making me create an account first, I'd actually try it."

**Most surprising thing:** Even someone working in big tech admitted they never seriously compared their AI subscriptions side by side. The spending had become “background noise.”

**What it changed about my design:** Reinforced the importance of fast onboarding and instant value. I decided the audit should work without login and immediately show potential savings before asking for any user details.

## Interview 3 — Software Tester at Coursera, May 12

**Background:** Works as a software tester in the edtech space and regularly tests AI-powered learning and productivity features. Frequently interacts with users adopting AI tools for studying, coding practice, resumes, and daily workflows.

**Quotes:**

> "Students and working professionals are buying premium AI subscriptions because everyone online says you need them to stay competitive."

> "A lot of users don't even know the difference between API usage and subscriptions — they just keep paying monthly."

> "The simpler the explanation, the more trustworthy the product feels."

**Most surprising thing:** Many non-technical users assume more expensive AI plans automatically mean better productivity, even when they barely use the tools.

**What it changed about my design:** Made me simplify the language in the audit report. Instead of technical explanations, the results page should first give a clear recommendation like: “You could save ₹4,000/month by removing unused overlap.”
